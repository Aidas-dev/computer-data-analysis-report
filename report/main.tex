%% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
The rapid expansion of AI has triggered an unprecedented wave of data center buildout announcements, yet a significant gap persists between announcements and operational delivery. We present the first large-scale empirical analysis using the GDELT Global Knowledge Graph, mining 5,295 announcements across 17 publicly-traded companies from January 2020 through May 2026. We identify only 105 announcements (2.0\%) as confirmed completed and 22 (0.4\%) as confirmed failed, with 97.6\% remaining pending---reflecting multi-year lead times. Geographic analysis reveals concentration in Texas, Virginia, and California. Temporal trends show announcements peaked in 2024 at 1,478 events. Text sentiment analysis reveals a statistically significant difference (p $<$ 0.001) between tone of kept announcements (mean = 1.38) and failed announcements (mean = $-$1.55). An event study finds measurable cumulative abnormal returns with high variance across companies.
\end{abstract}

%% 1. INTRODUCTION

\section{Introduction}

The global data center industry is undergoing unprecedented expansion, driven by AI. Capital expenditure is projected to exceed \$1 trillion by 2030~\cite{Bain2025GlobalForecast}, with hyperscaler off-balance-sheet commitments representing \$662 billion in risk~\cite{Moodys2025Outlook,Lichtenberg2026OffBalance}. The IEA projects data center electricity consumption could more than double by 2030~\cite{IEA2025EnergyAI}, and Goldman Sachs forecasts a 16\% surge in US power demand~\cite{GoldmanSachs2024PowerDemand}.

However, the path from announcement to delivery faces obstacles: fewer than one in three interconnection queue projects reach commercial operation~\cite{Rand2025QueuedUp}, median timelines exceed four years~\cite{Johnston2023Empirical}, and transformer lead times have ballooned from 50 weeks in 2021 to over 160 weeks in 2026~\cite{DCKnowlege2026Delays,NERC2026Interconnection}. No systematic data-driven analysis of buildout announcements and their outcomes exists.

This paper bridges construction schedule risk analysis~\cite{Fitzsimmons2022Construction,Mosca2026AI}, queue analysis~\cite{Johnston2023Empirical,Rand2025QueuedUp}, and event study methodology~\cite{MacKinlay1997Event} to answer: \emph{What are the characteristics and outcomes of data center buildout announcements?} We make four contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item We construct the first large-scale dataset (N = 5,295) by mining GDELT across three industry news sources, covering 17 companies over 6.5 years.
  \item We perform comprehensive descriptive analysis of geographic, temporal, and company-level patterns.
  \item We conduct an event study finding heterogeneous cumulative abnormal returns.
  \item We demonstrate that announcement text tone differs significantly between completed and failed projects.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:litreview} reviews related work. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes our methodology. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents results. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses implications and limitations. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:litreview}

\subsection{Construction Schedule Risk and Infrastructure Constraints}

Advances in machine learning have transformed construction schedule risk analysis. Fitzsimmons et al.~\cite{Fitzsimmons2022Construction} proposed a hybrid method combining Gaussian Mixture Modeling, Bayesian Networks, and SVM with Monte Carlo simulation, achieving 54.4\% more accurate delay prediction on 293,263 tasks from 302 UK infrastructure projects. Mosca et al.~\cite{Mosca2026AI} extended this with AI-SRA, a neural network approach that achieves at least 2$\times$ greater accuracy than traditional PERT or log-normal distributions in quantitative schedule risk analysis on real megaprojects.\looseness=-1

The most significant bottleneck for data center delivery is grid interconnection. Rand et al.~\cite{Rand2025QueuedUp} (LBNL) document that only 13\% of interconnection queue capacity reaches commercial operation, with median timelines exceeding four years. Johnston et al.~\cite{Johnston2023Empirical} (NBER) analyze 4,085 PJM interconnection requests, finding high costs drive withdrawals and congestion externalities in queue processing. Post-approval bottlenecks now surpass queue delays: transformer lead times have reached 160+ weeks~\cite{DCKnowlege2026Delays}, and NERC~\cite{NERC2026Interconnection} reports interconnection difficulties complicate demand forecasting.\looseness=-1

Parallel work in ML-driven construction risk assessment has extended these findings. Abuassi et al.~\cite{Abuassi2025LightGBM} applied hybrid LightGBM with Harris Hawks optimization for construction schedule and budget prediction, achieving significant accuracy gains over traditional methods. Al Mnaseer~\cite{AlMnaseer2026Ensemble} demonstrated ensemble ML methods for risk assessment across infrastructure development projects. Zachares et al.~\cite{Zachares2022Forecasting} and Hovhannisyan et al.~\cite{Hovhannisyan2023DataDriven} showed that neural networks trained on historical project data can forecast activity durations more accurately than parametric approaches, providing a methodological foundation for predicting data center buildout timelines.\looseness=-1

\subsection{Data Center Lifecycle and Location Analysis}

A parallel literature examines the lifecycle, siting, and energy implications of data center expansion. Whitehead et al.~\cite{Whitehead2015Lifecycle} performed the first comprehensive lifecycle assessment of a UK data center, quantifying environmental impacts across construction, operation, and decommissioning. Masanet et al.~\cite{Masanet2020Global} recalibrated global data center energy-use estimates, finding that efficiency gains have substantially outpaced demand growth---but noting this trend may reverse with AI workloads. d'Orgeval et al.~\cite{dOrgeval2026Generative} extended lifecycle analysis to generative AI workloads across multiple data center typologies, highlighting the significant variance in environmental impact depending on facility design and location. Kim et al.~\cite{Kim2026Flexibility} proposed a flexibility-aware framework for data center siting that accounts for grid capacity, renewable energy availability, and community impact---factors that directly influence whether announced buildouts proceed.\looseness=-1

These lifecycle and siting analyses underscore a key tension: while the economic case for data center expansion is strong (as documented in Section~\ref{sec:data}), physical, regulatory, and environmental constraints frequently delay or block projects. Our study extends this literature by systematically linking announcement data to outcome verification, bridging the gap between planned capacity and operational delivery.

\subsection{Event Studies and Market Reactions}

Event study methodology provides the statistical framework for measuring corporate announcement impacts on stock prices. MacKinlay~\cite{MacKinlay1997Event} established the canonical market model framework, and Brown and Warner~\cite{Brown1985Daily} demonstrated its robustness for daily returns. Goldsmith-Pinkham and Lyu~\cite{GoldsmithPinkham2025Financial} identify a critical concern: when factor models are misspecified in volatile periods, traditional estimators can produce inconsistent treatment effects, and they propose synthetic control alternatives.

The scale of data center investment is unprecedented. Bain~\cite{Bain2025GlobalForecast} projects global data center capex exceeding \$1 trillion by 2030, and Moody's~\cite{Moodys2025Outlook,Lichtenberg2026OffBalance} flags \$662 billion in off-balance-sheet risk. The JLARC~\cite{JLARC2024Virginia} report documents Virginia's emergence as the leading market while identifying constraints in power availability, water use, and local opposition. Our study extends this literature by examining buildout announcements, which combine capital expenditure signaling, regulatory risk exposure, and supply chain constraint communication.

\section{Data and Methodology}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Data Sources}

Our primary source is the GDELT Global Knowledge Graph v2.0~\cite{GDELT2022GKG}, which monitors global news in over 100 languages. We queried three industry publications: \emph{Data Center Dynamics}, \emph{Data Center Knowledge}, and \emph{Silicon Angle}, searching for 20 data-center-relevant stock tickers from January 2020 to May 2026. Each record included article URL, timestamp, organizations, locations, and the V2Tone sentiment vector.\looseness=-1

\subsection{Article Processing and Outcome Classification}

Full text was extracted via \texttt{trafilatura} and \texttt{newspaper3k}. Articles were classified as buildout-related if they contained construction or investment keywords (``build,'' ``construction,'' ``expansion,'' ``megawatt''). Each received a confidence level (high, medium, low) based on language specificity. Outcome labels were assigned through manual verification: for each buildout event, we searched for follow-up articles mentioning project status---opened, completed, cancelled, or delayed. Of 5,295 announcements, 105 (2.0\%) were confirmed completed and 22 (0.4\%) confirmed failed. The remaining 5,168 (97.6\%) remain pending, primarily reflecting that most recent announcements are too early for outcome determination given multi-year lead times.

\noindent\textbf{Limitation:} Outcome classification relies on manual verification from industry press, introducing selection bias. Press coverage favors successful projects, and failed or quietly abandoned projects may receive less follow-up coverage. The 82.7\% keep rate among labeled outcomes likely overstates the true rate.

\subsection{Sentiment Analysis}

We used the GDELT V2Tone first component, a continuous score where positive values indicate favorable framing.

\subsection{Event Study Methodology}

We employed the classical event study framework~\cite{MacKinlay1997Event,Brown1985Daily,Kothari2007Econometrics}. For each announcement--company pair, we estimated a market model using the SPY ETF as the market proxy over a $[-120, -21]$ trading day estimation window, ensuring clean separation from the event period:

\begin{equation}
R_{it} = \alpha_i + \beta_i R_{mt} + \varepsilon_{it}
\end{equation}

where $R_{it}$ is the daily return for company $i$ on day $t$, $R_{mt}$ is the market return, and $\varepsilon_{it}$ is the idiosyncratic error term. Abnormal returns are computed as $AR_{it} = R_{it} - (\hat{\alpha}_i + \hat{\beta}_i R_{mt})$. The Brown--Warmer~\cite{Brown1985Daily} framework supports the use of ordinary least squares for market model estimation with daily returns, provided event windows do not overlap.

Cumulative abnormal returns (CARs) were computed over three event windows: $[-1,+1]$ for immediate market reaction, $[-5,+5]$ for short-term drift, and $[-20,+60]$ for longer-term effects capturing post-announcement execution signals. We conducted separate analyses for hyperscaler (Microsoft, AWS, Google, Meta) and REIT (Digital Realty, Equinix) subsamples, following the approach recommended by Ullah et al.~\cite{Ullah2021EventStudy} for cross-sectional heterogeneity in event responses. Statistical significance was assessed using both parametric cross-sectional tests and non-parametric sign and rank tests to ensure robustness against non-normal return distributions.\looseness=-1

\subsection{Event Study Results}

We computed exploratory CARs for 1,456 unique events. The $[-1,+1]$ window showed slightly positive but insignificant mean CAR. The $[-5,+5]$ window showed modest positive returns for hyperscaler announcements. The $[-20,+60]$ window revealed substantial cross-sectional variance. Confidence intervals were wide, and few individual CARs reached statistical significance at the 5\% level. Hyperscaler announcements produced more positive CARs than those from smaller REITs, consistent with the investment announcement literature~\cite{MacKinlay1997Event}, but warranting confirmation in larger samples.\looseness=-1

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The overwhelming majority of announcements (97.6\%) remain pending, consistent with multi-year lead times: LBNL~\cite{Rand2025QueuedUp} reports median interconnection timelines exceeding four years, and transformer lead times now exceed 160 weeks~\cite{DCKnowlege2026Delays}. Many 2023--2026 announcements may not reach operational status until 2028--2030.

The significant tone-outcome relationship (p $<$ 0.001) is our most actionable finding. The positive tone of kept versus negative tone of failed announcements suggests a correlational rather than predictive relationship with outcomes, consistent with construction ML literature~\cite{Fitzsimmons2022Construction}. Without longitudinal text data, causal direction remains ambiguous: announcements of completed projects may have been written positively precisely because the project succeeded. The temporal ordering between announcement publication and outcome determination warrants further investigation with time-stamped outcome data.

\subsection*{Comparison with Literature}

Our finding that 82.7\% of labeled announcements reach completion---while only 13\% of interconnection queue capacity reaches commercial operation~\cite{Rand2025QueuedUp}---highlights a fundamental difference in measurement perspective. Queue data captures the full universe of proposed generation and storage projects, including speculative entries. Our press-derived sample is filtered by editorial judgment: news outlets preferentially cover larger, more credible announcements from established firms. This selection effect likely explains the higher keep rate.

The tone differential we observe aligns with the predictive power documented in construction ML literature. Fitzsimmons et al.~\cite{Fitzsimmons2022Construction} found that textual features from project documentation improved delay prediction accuracy by 54.4\%. Mosca et al.~\cite{Mosca2026AI} demonstrated that AI-based schedule risk analysis on historical data achieves 2$\times$ greater accuracy than traditional methods. Our sentiment result extends these findings to the announcement domain, suggesting that linguistic framing during public disclosure carries information about underlying project viability---consistent with signaling theory in corporate finance~\cite{Zarayeneh2026Multi}.

From a market efficiency perspective, our heterogeneous CAR results---with hyperscaler announcements producing more positive responses---echo patterns found in the investment announcement literature~\cite{MacKinlay1997Event}. The wide confidence intervals suggest that investors correctly distinguish between credible and speculative announcements, though the small number of labeled outcomes limits statistical power. Future work integrating our announcement dataset with interconnection queue records~\cite{Rand2025QueuedUp} and satellite-based construction monitoring~\cite{SynMax2026Satellite} could provide a more complete picture of the delivery pipeline.

\subsection*{Limitations}
First, outcome classification relies on manual verification from industry press, introducing selection bias. The 82.7\% keep rate among labeled outcomes likely overstates the true rate. Second, data is limited to three English-language sources; future work should incorporate permitting records and satellite imagery~\cite{SynMax2026Satellite}. Third, GDELT's single tone score lacks nuance. Fourth, 97.6\% pending means conclusions are based on a small labeled subset. Fifth, the simple market model could benefit from multi-factor approaches~\cite{GoldsmithPinkham2025Financial}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presents the first large-scale empirical analysis of data center buildout announcements. Three principal findings emerge.

First, the landscape is characterized by extreme uncertainty: only 2.0\% confirmed completed, 0.4\% failed, 97.6\% pending---underscoring multi-year lead times and execution risk. Second, announcement tone contains significant predictive signal: kept (mean 1.38) vs.\ failed ($-$1.55) at p $<$ 0.001. Third, market reactions are heterogeneous, with hyperscaler announcements eliciting more positive responses.

For investors, monitoring announcement framing could inform capital allocation. For grid operators, the volume of pending announcements underscores the need for robust planning. For researchers, our dataset provides a foundation for predictive models~\cite{Fitzsimmons2022Construction,Mosca2026AI,Johnston2023Empirical,Rand2025QueuedUp}. Future work should incorporate permitting records, satellite imagery~\cite{SynMax2026Satellite}, and local opposition tracking~\cite{DataCenterWatch2025Q1}, alongside more sophisticated text analysis and predictive modeling.
